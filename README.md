# electron-positron-annihilation
Python code that analyzes the experimental data obtained from electron–positron annihilation. It calculates the statistical uncertainties of the measured coincidence counts and plots them as a function of the angle between the detectors.

# Description 
Electron–positron annihilation is a fundamental process in which an electron interacts with a positron, resulting in the conversion of their mass into electromagnetic radiation. At low kinetic energies, the process produces two gamma-ray photons with an energy of 511 keV each.
The two photons are emitted in opposite directions as a consequence of momentum conservation. This characteristic angular correlation provides the experimental signature of electron–positron annihilation.

# Experimental Setup
In this experiment, a radioactive sodium-22 source is used to produce positrons. The positrons are slowed down in the surrounding material and subsequently annihilate with electrons, producing the characteristic gamma radiation.
Two NaI detectors are placed around the source and used to detect the emitted photons. Their signals are amplified and processed by the electronic system, while a coincidence circuit records photons detected simultaneously by both detectors. The measurement time is set to 100 seconds.
By changing the angle between the detectors and recording the corresponding coincidence counts, the angular correlation of the annihilation photons is experimentally investigated.
